\textit{The Septuagint (LXX) version of Daniel is not the earliest Greek form
  of the book; rather, it is largely a later revision that replaced an
  older Greek translation known as the Old Greek (OG). The OG version
  of Book of Daniel differs noticeably in wording, order, and
  sometimes content, often being freer and more paraphrastic, while
  the LXX (in practice, the revision associated with Theodotion) is
  closer to the Hebrew/Aramaic text and more literal. Although the historical
  Theodotion is usually dated to the 2nd century AD, commonly around AD 150–180,
  there is evidence that this ``Theodotionic'' version of Daniel is based on a
  much earlier revision that already existed in the first century BC or first
  century AD and the New Testament sometimes reflects readings closer to
  Theodotionic tradition than to OG Daniel. The OG
  text was mostly supplanted in Christian
  tradition by the Theodotionic/LXX form, though the OG survives in
  some manuscripts and is important for textual criticism. In this
  edition an AI-generated translation of the Old Greek appears in the
  third column labelled (OG). (The AI translation has not been manually checked and
  may contain errors.)}
